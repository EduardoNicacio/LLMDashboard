Final Testing Notes

Observations
Compiled by the lab after the 200-prompt session

Qwen3.6 27B · Takeaways

  • Surprisingly strong on multi-step coding prompts — passed 18/20 of the SWE-bench-style mini-set with one shot.
  • VRAM ceiling hit at 18.4GB during 4K context runs; drops to 14.1GB at 2K. Recommend 4K cap for stability.
  • Creative writing has a slight "model voice" tell — overuses em-dashes in long-form fiction. Minor.
  • Quantization (Q4_K_M) is the sweet spot: Q5 loses ~0.3 quality, Q3 loses ~0.9. Stick with Q4.
  • Best local 27B-class model we've tested to date — beats Llama 3.3 70B Q4 on coding at 1/3 the VRAM.

Claude Opus 4.8 · Takeaways

  • Cleanest instruction following of any model tested — JSON schema adherence 99.4%.
  • Streaming TTFT consistently under 400ms — best-in-class responsiveness.
  • Hallucination rate 1.8% on factual recall set — only Gemini 2.0 Pro is lower (1.4%).
  • At scale ($47.82 / 200 prompts) it's expensive for hobbyist use — break-even vs local hardware is ~14 weeks of daily 4-hour use.
  • Refusal calibration is the most "human-feeling" — won't lecture, won't over-refuse, gets nuance right.

Methodology

200 prompts across 6 categories (coding, reasoning, creative, instruction-following, factual, conversation). Each prompt run 3 times per model with temperature 0.7 for stochastic tasks, 0.0 for deterministic. Grading done by an independent GPT-judge panel of 3 with a fourth tiebreaker. Local runs on a single RTX 3090 with llama.cpp b3042; frontier runs through official Anthropic API. All timings measured server-side.

Final Recommendation

For most creators and developers, Qwen3.6 27B is the sweet spot — frontier-adjacent quality with zero marginal cost and full privacy. Reach for Claude Opus 4.8 when the task demands strict instruction following, nuanced long-form reasoning, or you're hitting context windows past 8K. Keep both in your stack.
